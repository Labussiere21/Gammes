Learning the notes on the fretboard

Knowing where every note is on the neck is what lets you find a root instantly, read a chord chart and move a scale or an arpeggio to any key. This trainer asks you one note at a time: either you find it on a given string, or a position lights up and you name it.

A method that works

Start with the natural notes on frets 0 to 5, one string at a time. On every string, the open note comes back at the 12th fret, and there is only a half step between E and F and between B and C: those two pairs are your landmarks. Then add strings, widen to fret 12, and only then switch on the sharps and flats, which are simply the frets in between.

Five minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Use the 60-second session to measure your progress: when you reach about thirty correct answers a minute on the whole neck, the notes have become reflexes.

Octave shapes

Once you know a note on one string, octaves give you the others: two strings up and two frets up is the same note one octave higher (three frets when you cross the B string on guitar). From the E string you get the D string, from the A string the G string.
