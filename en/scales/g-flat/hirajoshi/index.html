About this scale

Hirajoshi is one of the best-known Japanese pentatonic scales. With its two characteristic semitones, it creates a contemplative, melancholic atmosphere typical of traditional Japanese music (koto, shakuhachi). Widely used in contemporary music and alternative metal.

Which chord to play it over

The chord built on the root is G♭m: this is where the scale sounds most at home.

How to practise this scale

  1. Play the scale up and down slowly with a metronome (60 bpm, one note per beat). Say each note name out loud.
  2. Play it in three-note groups (1-2-3, 2-3-4…) and then skipping every other note, to break out of the up-and-down run.
  3. Play the G♭m arpeggio (root, third, fifth), then go back to the scale and land on those notes on the strong beats.
  4. Improvise over a sustained G♭m chord or a looped G♭ drone: start and end your phrases on a chord tone.
  5. Move to other keys around the cycle of fourths: the same shape slides along the neck unchanged.
