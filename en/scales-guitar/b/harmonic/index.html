About this scale

The harmonic minor scale gives guitar a dramatic, Eastern-tinged sound that is instantly recognizable. Its augmented second (W+H) between the 6th and 7th degrees creates a distinctive leap found in flamenco, neoclassical metal (Yngwie Malmsteen) and film scores. It sounds magnificent over i–V progressions in all minor keys.

Which chord to play it over

The chord built on the root is BmMaj7: this is where the scale sounds most at home.

Compared with B natural minor, what changes: 7 (A♯). These are the notes to bring out so the colour of the scale comes through.

How to practise this scale

  1. Play the scale up and down slowly with a metronome (60 bpm, one note per beat). Say each note name out loud.
  2. Play it in thirds (1-3, 2-4, 3-5…) and then in three-note groups: this is what turns a scale into phrases.
  3. Play the BmMaj7 arpeggio (root, third, fifth, seventh), then go back to the scale and land on those notes on the strong beats.
  4. Improvise over a sustained BmMaj7 chord or a looped B drone, leaning on the characteristic notes.
  5. Move to other keys around the cycle of fourths: the same shape slides along the neck unchanged.
